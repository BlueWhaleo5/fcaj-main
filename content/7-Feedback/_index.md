### Overall Evaluation

**1. Program Structure**
The First Cloud AI Journey program strikes a good balance between a self-driven project (ServerlessFinance, built and iterated across 8 phases) and community touchpoints (AWS Study Group, in-person events). Being able to design the project's own scope and pace, while still having a real deadline and real AWS infrastructure to answer to, made the learning feel earned rather than handed over.

**2. Community Events**
The events attended over the course of the program (FCAJ Community Day, AgentForge - Ho Chi Minh City, FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week, and AgentForge - Deep Dive) were consistently high quality and hands-on rather than purely promotional. AgentForge - Ho Chi Minh City in particular (a full-day, L300-400 deep dive into Amazon Bedrock AgentCore) was the most technically substantial session attended; it directly shaped how I think about the access-control gap in ServerlessFinance's own API.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Major**
Building ServerlessFinance touched on distributed systems, cloud infrastructure-as-code, and performance engineering (the Rust/PyO3 hot path) relevant to a Artificial Intelligence background, while also going well beyond typical coursework into real production concerns: cost management, observability, and security boundaries (API keys, IAM least privilege).

**4. Learning & Skill Development Opportunities**
The project format forced learning-by-building rather than learning-by-tutorial: several real problems came up that had no clean textbook answer (a planned AWS service being unavailable in the target region, a Lambda concurrency quota blocking a distributed workload, a Rust toolchain issue inside a Docker multi-stage build) and had to be root-caused and fixed directly against the AWS account. That kind of debugging experience is hard to get any other way.

**5. Community Culture**
Publishing to the AWS Study Group and seeing the blog get read and reacted to made the "share what you learn" culture feel real. The AgentForge workshop, similarly, had a strong practitioner-sharing culture (community speakers from multiple companies, not just AWS staff) that made the content feel grounded in real production experience rather than generic marketing material.

---

### Additional Questions
- **Most satisfying part of the program:** the mentors were genuinely dedicated, and the AWS Builders supporting the program were consistently helpful, regularly checking in on how participants were doing. The program was also a place to take knowledge that had only ever lived in textbooks and actually apply it the way a real business would.
- **What could be improved for future participants:** the office-visit schedule should be distributed more evenly across universities — as it stood, students ended up competing for a limited number of office slots, which made it harder to keep up attendance.
- **Would I recommend this program to a friend:** Of course. The combination of a real, self-scoped AWS project and a genuine community (dedicated mentors, events, study group) is a rare mix for an internship program.

---

### Suggestions & Expectations
- Add project review sessions across teams/universities, or a dedicated exchange day per university, so participants can learn more from each other.
- Bring in more speakers from industry to share their experience, paired with things like a job fair or mock interviews, to connect the program more directly to real job opportunities.
- I hope to stay engaged with the program through all 6 tracks, not just the internship portion.
